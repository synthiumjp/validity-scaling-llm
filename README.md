> ## Correction notice (November 2026)
>
> The three April 2026 preprints built on this repository (arXiv:2604.17707, 2604.17714 and 2604.17716) contained errors that change their headline results. They are replaced by one corrected paper, **arXiv:2604.17714v2**, and 2604.17707 and 2604.17716 are being withdrawn.
>
> - **DeepSeek-R1 was not "inverted".** 71 of its 72 responses on the free-text (prospective) track were declines, which the harness logged as KEEP+BET and the grader scored as errors. The reported r = −.80, RBS = +.87 and the "85% → 11% accuracy at 10% coverage" collapse came from those rows. Corrected, R1 withdraws 95% of its correct answers (blanket withdrawal).
> - **The free-text track is mis-graded** by substring matching, in both directions. It is excluded from the primary analysis.
> - **The published tiers were not produced by the screening tool** as released. The corrected tool is `validity-screen` 0.3.0.
> - **The selective-prediction "criterion validation"** used a criterion computed from the same items and probe as the screen. It is reported only descriptively.
> - **The Yang et al. (2024) reanalysis was not computed from that study's data.** The hard-coded results table has been removed and replaced by an analysis of the released predictions.
>
> Everything in the v1 papers that depends on these points should be treated as superseded. Version 1 materials are kept in `analysis/` and `figures/Paper */` for the record.

## The paper

**Cacioli, J. P. (2026). Screen Before You Interpret: A Portable Validity Screen for Benchmark-Based LLM Confidence Signals.** arXiv:2604.17714v2. Source and PDF: [`paper/v2/`](paper/v2/).

The screen computes, from the correctness-by-confidence table, the rate of high confidence on errors (L), the rate of low confidence on correct answers (Fp) and their contrast (RBS = −Youden's J). It separates blanket confidence, blanket withdrawal and inversion, attaches interval guards, and calls a signal Valid only when it measurably tracks correctness.

Main results (all regenerated by `analysis/v2/run_all.sh`):

- **Battery, binary KEEP/WITHDRAW probe** (20 models, five keyed tracks, 452 items): 1 Invalid (DeepSeek-R1, blanket withdrawal), 4 Indeterminate, 15 Valid. Two Indeterminate models (Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen3 Next 80B Thinking) keep confidence on 96% of errors but make too few errors to certify it.
- **Sample size:** certifying blanket confidence needs 35 errors at L = 1.00, 71 at .97, 97 at .96 and 139 at .95. Only 6 of 20 battery verdicts reproduce reliably on half the items.
- **Verbalized 0–100 confidence** (33 atlas models on 1,500 MMLU items; 20 models on a separate 500-item MMLU sample; Yang et al.'s 11 models × 10 benchmarks): the verdict depends on the cut point. At 50 most models are Invalid because confidence piles at the ceiling; at 90–95 most pass.
- **Verdicts do not transfer between probe formats.** Battery item sensitivity does not predict MMLU AUROC (ρ = −.07). Gemma 3 1B, 12B and 27B pass the binary screen and fail on MMLU at cut points 50 and 90.

## Data

120 CSVs from the metacognitive monitoring battery (Cacioli, 2026c; arXiv:2604.15702). Each holds per-item responses for one model on one track: correctness, the KEEP/WITHDRAW probe and the BET/NO BET probe. In the five two-alternative tracks the files contain the answer key but not the model's response text, so non-answers there cannot be audited. The prospective track contains free-text answers graded by substring match; see the correction notice above.
